Reflection

When I started this project, I had a clear vision of creating a festival that could appeal to everyone, thinking that a broad approach would naturally gather as many people as possible. However, this quickly turned out to be the biggest turning point in my process. Through early user testing, feedback sessions, and the insights I gained during the first couple of lectures, I realized that trying to attract an entire age group with one generic concept simply wasn’t realistic. If you try to speak to everyone, you end up resonating with no one. Looking back at my early portfolio entries, I can clearly see how broad and unfocused my initial ideas were. The moment everything shifted was when I decided to stop aiming for mass appeal and instead lean heavily into my persona, Emma. By focusing specifically on her needs and motivations, I reshaped the entire concept. Rather than selling the festival merely on the promise of learning a new skill, I pivoted toward creating a space focused on finding genuine human connections through a shared hobby. This narrowed focus gave the festival a much stronger identity and purpose.

Testing and feedback also revealed blind spots in my visual communication that I would never have discovered on my own. While the concept made complete sense in my head, my testers pointed out that the visual direction of the festival wasn’t clear at all. Specifically, many people misinterpreted the bright color palette in my branding as something childish or overly digital, which clashed with the grounded, offline experience I wanted to promote. Based on this feedback, I realized I needed to anchor the visual identity in something more tangible and warm. I introduced new marketing materials featuring a bonfire and refined my moodboards and overall aesthetic to better communicate the cozy, real-world atmosphere of the event. Once I made those adjustments, the feedback immediately shifted in the right direction. Despite the initial confusion, I deliberately chose not to change the bright colors themselves. It is my festival, and to me, those vibrant tones represent something new, growing, and exciting—qualities that are essential to the spirit of trying a new hobby and meeting new people.

In my next project, I will definitely make much more active use of both AI and feedback from real people right from the initial idea stage. Going into this project, I assumed that growing digital dependency was universally seen as a major problem that people actively wanted to escape. However, throughout my research and testing, it became clear that most people don’t actually view it as a crisis, but rather as an inevitable part of an ever-evolving world. Because my core premise didn’t align with how people actually felt, it was difficult to build a festival spirit that people took seriously. Testing that core assumption earlier would have saved me a lot of guesswork and helped me frame the festival’s cause in a much more relatable way.
